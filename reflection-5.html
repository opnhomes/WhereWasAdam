The Gap · Part V · Parish & Faith

Where Are the Men?

On visible faith, and the men our parishes and families are waiting for.

“If it is displeasing to you to serve the LORD, choose today whom you will serve… As for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.” Joshua 24:15

Here’s an invitation before anything else, and it’s meant to be real: picture your family right now — not someday, not the story you’ll tell years from now. The actual scene, this week, this Sunday. Are you visibly in it, leading them toward God, or standing off to the side?

Look at the pews.

Walk into almost any parish on a Sunday and count. The women, the children, the older couples, and then the men — scattered, fewer, often at the back, often first out the door. This isn’t an insult; it’s a pattern most of us have seen our whole lives, and one we’ve probably been part of. The gap we’ve been naming for four weeks — between the man we’re called to be and the man we’ve been — shows up here in public, where anyone can see it. And the people watching most closely are the ones who live with us.

The first Adam failed in a garden. The New Adam knelt in one.

In the Garden of Eden, Adam stood beside his wife while the serpent talked and said nothing. In the Garden of Gethsemane, the New Adam went to the same kind of place and did the opposite. He took the cup, knelt down, and prayed: “Father, if you are willing, take this cup away from me; still, not my will but yours be done” (Luke 22:42). Then he turned to the three men he’d brought with him and asked the question that sits under this whole week: “So you could not keep watch with me for one hour?” (Matthew 26:40). One hour. Not a crusade. Not a sacrifice. Just to be awake and present, with him. We’ve all fallen asleep in that garden. The invitation is to get up.

Faith was never meant to be delegated.

It’s easy to assume faith is something the parish handles, or our wives handle, or the priest handles, while we handle the “real world.” The Church teaches the opposite: the family is the “domestic church,” the first place faith is handed on, and a father is the first man a child ever watches pray or not pray. St. Louis Martin, father of St. Thérèse of Lisieux, is a quiet example. He made daily Mass, family prayer, and the Sunday rest part of the rhythm of his household, and the faith of his daughters grew up inside that rhythm. He never ran a program. He just went first, and let them watch.

Joshua said it out loud, in front of everyone.

Joshua stood before the whole people of Israel and made it plain: choose today. Then he went first. “As for me and my household, we will serve the LORD” (Joshua 24:15). He didn’t wait for the crowd to decide, and he didn’t make it a private resolution. He said it where his family could hear. St. Paul gives the same charge to every man: “Be on your guard, stand firm in the faith, be courageous, be strong. Your every act should be done with love” (1 Corinthians 16:13–14). Courage and love in the same breath. Not loud, not performative. Visible.

An open door.

None of this is about guilt over lost time. It’s an open door. Your parish doesn’t need one more volunteer sign-up sheet. It needs men willing to kneel down and mean it, in front of the people who are watching to see if it’s real — and that door is open to you starting this week, whatever came before it.

Week 5 Challenge · Parish & Faith

Where are the men — and am I one of them?

Pick one visible, specific act of faith your family will actually see this week — not a private resolution, an observable one. Go to confession. Lead your family in prayer. Show up 30 minutes early to Mass. Let them watch you go first.
