The Gap · Part III · Marriage

Loving Sacrificially

On the vow that was spoken once, and has to be lived daily.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ loved the church and handed himself over for her.” Ephesians 5:25

Last week we named the war inside. This week we turn to sacrificial love, honoring the sacrament of marriage, and understanding our bodies.

For married men, we married her to love her — fully, sacrificially, the way Christ loves the Church. That calling didn’t expire at the reception. This is about what it looks like to show up for our wives, each day, week, month, and year, no matter the cost. For unmarried men, it’s about growing in understanding of this sacrament.

Tobiah modeled marital chastity.

When the stakes couldn’t be higher for him, Tobiah shows us how to honor our wives. Tobiah doesn’t treat Sarah as something to use. She has already lost seven husbands to a demon (Tobit 3:8), and he does what the angel Raphael told him: he burns the fish’s liver and heart in the bridal chamber, the demon flees, and Raphael pursues it and “bound it hand and foot” (Tobit 8:2–3). Chastity in marriage is never passive. It’s a fight we are called to as guardians of our wives. While Adam failed to protect Eve from the serpent, Tobiah drives away a demon. He gets up, calls her to pray with him, and the two of them ask God for mercy and protection (Tobit 8:4–8). He blesses God out loud, recalls Eden itself — God making Eve as a companion for Adam — and says plainly: “Now, not with lust, but with fidelity I take this kinswoman as my wife” (Tobit 8:7). That is loving her with fidelity, not lust.

Our bodies were made to say something too.

St. John Paul II spent years unpacking one insight in his Theology of the Body: our bodies aren’t just biological facts, they carry meaning — what he called their “nuptial” or spousal meaning. A husband’s body isn’t built for taking; it’s built for total, faithful, fruitful self-gift. It echoes a line from Vatican II that he returned to again and again: man “cannot fully find himself except through a sincere gift of himself” (Gaudium et Spes, 24).

That’s the shape of Ephesians 5:25. Christ didn’t love the Church in theory. He handed Himself over. And Paul says the same pattern should govern the husband’s everyday care: nourishing and cherishing, as Christ does the Church (Ephesians 5:29). Our marriages are asking for that love in ordinary moments — in honoring our wives’ bodies, not seeing them as objects of pleasure, having hard conversations, the apology we don’t want to give.

For the unmarried man

If you’re not married, this week isn’t a pass. It’s preparation. Paul calls marriage “a great mystery” (Ephesians 5:32), because it points to Christ giving Himself for the Church. Whether God is calling you to marriage, to the priesthood, or to a single life given to Him, the lesson is the same: love is a gift of self, not something to take. Chastity doesn’t begin on the wedding night. It’s trained now, in what we do with our eyes, our time, and our attention. The thieves we named last week are the same ones that will follow us to the altar if we don’t deal with them first. So learn now to honor women as daughters of God, never as objects of pleasure. Pray for the vocation God has for you, and, if marriage is it, for the woman who may one day be your wife. And study the sacrament itself: a covenant, a mission, and a road to heaven walked together.

So — are we still the man she married?

Not “did we mean the vow” — of course we did. The question is whether love is still costing us something, or whether it’s quietly become comfortable, transactional, withheld. Where in our marriage have we gone as silent as Adam did, when what she actually needs is a man who prays over her like Tobiah and gives himself for her like Christ?

Week 3 Challenge · Marriage

Am I still showing up?

This week has two parts:

Part 1 — Ask her, and listen. Bring it to our Lord first, ideally before the Blessed Sacrament: where have I gone quiet in my marriage? The conversation I’ve avoided, the apology I owe, the attention that’s gone elsewhere. Then ask your wife one question this week: “Where do you most need me to show up right now?” Don’t defend. Don’t fix. Listen, thank her, and write down what she said. For unmarried men, ask our Lord where He is calling you to grow in sacrificial love.

Part 2 — Pray with her, out loud, like Tobiah. Once a day this week, even for sixty seconds. If it’s new or awkward, start simply: an Our Father, then a short prayer for her by name. If she isn’t ready to pray together, pray over her on your own. If you’re unmarried, ask our Lord what faithful love looks like now.

The goal isn’t a grand gesture. It’s a costly, ordinary yes, repeated daily, the way the vow was meant to be lived.
