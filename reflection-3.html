Week 3  —  Oct 25–31

You're Still the Man She Married

On the vow that was spoken once, and has to be lived daily.

"Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her." Ephesians 5:25

We married her to love her — fully, sacrificially, the way Christ loved the Church. That calling didn't expire at the reception. This is about what it looks like to show up for our wives, each day, week, month, and year, no matter the cost. This is our vocation.

We already know how to speak. The question is whether we will.

Adam wasn't mute. The first words recorded from any man in Scripture are about his wife: "bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh" (Genesis 2:23) — a man overjoyed, a man who spoke up. The tragedy of Genesis 3 isn't that Adam never had words for her. It's that he had them once, and then, at the one moment they were most needed, he went quiet instead. That's the danger in marriage specifically: not that we never speak the vow, but that we stop speaking — engaging, leading, protecting — once the wedding is behind us and the routine sets in.

Tobias didn't go silent on his wedding night

Tobit gives us the opposite move, in almost the same setting Adam failed in — a bridal chamber, a new wife, a moment charged with everything a marriage will become. But the story is bigger than a single prayer. Sarah had already lost seven husbands, each one killed on their wedding night by the demon Asmodeus (Tobit 3:8) — she wasn't just a bride, she was a woman guarded by real danger, in a moment that had already proven fatal to every man before Tobias. He doesn't walk in unaware or unprepared. Following the angel Raphael's instruction, Tobias burns the heart and liver of a fish on the wedding night — a deliberate, physical act of protection — and the demon flees, is pursued, and is bound (Tobit 8:1–3). Adam, when the danger came for Eve, did nothing. Tobias, when the danger came for Sarah, acted first, protected her, and only then went to her.

And even then, he doesn't treat her as something to use. Before anything else, he calls her "sister" (Tobit 8:4) — the word itself is restraint, a refusal to reduce her to what the moment could have been about. Then he takes her hand, and prays with her, out loud, naming what marriage is actually for — going back to Eden itself, to God giving Eve to Adam as a companion, not an object (Tobit 8:6). He asks God's protection over their life together, not because of lust, but for a true and faithful union (Tobit 8:7). Only after the danger was faced, and the woman was honored, and the prayer was prayed — only then does the marriage become what it's for. Where Adam went silent in a garden and let the danger reach her unopposed, Tobias acts, protects, honors, and prays in a bedroom — the same category of moment, the opposite result on every count.

Our bodies were made to say something too

St. John Paul II spent years unpacking a single insight in his Theology of the Body: our bodies aren't just biological facts, they carry meaning — what he called their "nuptial" or spousal meaning. A husband's body isn't built for taking; it's built for total, faithful, fruitful self-gift, the same shape as Christ's gift of Himself on the Cross. That's not abstract theology — it's the direct explanation of why Ephesians 5:25 uses the Cross as the measuring stick for marital love.

Christ didn't love the Church in theory. He gave His body. Not because our marriages will ever be asked for that scale of sacrifice — but because they're asked for that same shape of it: total, not held back. Most days that's not a cross. It's the apology we don't want to give. The hard conversation we'd rather avoid. Presence on the night we'd rather disappear into a screen.

So — are we still the man she married?

Not "did we mean the vow" — of course we did. The question is whether love is still costing us something, or whether it's quietly become comfortable, transactional, withheld. Where in our marriage have we gone as silent as Adam did, when what she actually needs is a man who prays over her like Tobias and gives himself for her like Christ?
