The Gap · Part VII · Workplace

The Meeting Nobody Wanted to Run

On the silence we keep at work, and the Christian we’re called to be when it counts.

“Let your ‘Yes’ mean ‘Yes,’ and your ‘No’ mean ‘No.’”Matthew 5:37
Tip: use the ← → keys

Part 1 of 7Begin

Everyone has been in the meeting. Something is clearly wrong — a decision headed the wrong way, a colleague being treated unfairly, a number that doesn’t add up, a plan nobody believes in. Everyone in the room can feel it. And everyone, including us, looks at the table and says nothing. Then we walk out and say what we really thought in the hallway.

Part 2 of 7Adam was standing right there.

Go back to the Garden one more time. Genesis says the woman “took some of its fruit and ate it; and she gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it” (Genesis 3:6). Who was with her. Adam wasn’t off somewhere else. He was standing next to the conversation the whole time and said nothing. Then, when God asked what happened, he found his voice at last, and it came out as blame: “The woman whom you put here with me — she gave me fruit from the tree, so I ate it” (Genesis 3:12). Silence when it counts, and blame when it’s over. That’s the pattern, and most of us have worked in it for years.

Part 3 of 7The fear at the table.

It’s worth being honest about why we go quiet. It’s rarely cowardice in the dramatic sense. It’s the mortgage, the review cycle, the promotion, the reputation, the fear of being the guy who made it awkward. Those fears are real, and we shouldn’t pretend otherwise. But we should name what we’re choosing. Every time we stay silent, we’re deciding that keeping the peace is worth more than the truth. Over time that decision shapes us, and we become men whose “yes” and “no” no longer mean much. Jesus asks for something simpler and harder: “Let your ‘Yes’ mean ‘Yes,’ and your ‘No’ mean ‘No’” (Matthew 5:37).

Part 4 of 7Truth, in love — not as a crusade.

This isn’t a call to become the office crusader. St. Paul gives the balance: “living the truth in love, we should grow in every way into him who is the head, Christ” (Ephesians 4:15), and “putting away falsehood, speak the truth, each one to his neighbor, for we are members one of another” (Ephesians 4:25). Truth and love, held together. Often the faithful thing isn’t a speech. It’s a quiet word to a colleague before the meeting, a question that opens the door for someone else, an honest “I don’t think this is right,” said with respect, or a refusal to sign off on something we know is false.

Part 5 of 7The king’s good servant, and God’s first.

St. Thomas More spent his career in the highest levels of English government, as a lawyer, a judge, and Lord Chancellor, and he was good at it. He took the work seriously, served honestly, and was loved by many of the people he worked with. When the king demanded he put his conscience aside, More refused, quietly, without rebellion, and it cost him his life. On the scaffold he is reported to have said he died “the king’s good servant, and God’s first.” Most of us will never face anything like that. But the same choice shows up in smaller form every week at an ordinary desk.

Part 6 of 7The same man in every room.

We can’t be one man at Mass and another at the office. The New Adam was the same in the synagogue, at the table with sinners, and before Pilate. That’s what integrity actually means: one man, whole. Our colleagues don’t need us to preach. They need to see a man whose word can be trusted and whose faith is the same Monday morning as it is Sunday.

Part 7 of 7This Week’s Challenge

Week 7 Challenge · Workplace

What am I not saying — and why?

This week has two parts:

Part 1 — Say the unsaid sentence. Think of one thing at work that you know needs to be said and haven’t said: a concern, a correction, an honest assessment, an apology, a “no.” Pray about it first. Then say it this week, clearly, respectfully, and without an audience if possible.

Part 2 — Audit your yes and no. At the end of each day, ask: where did I say “yes” when I meant “no,” or stay quiet when I should have spoken? Write one line. By Friday you’ll see the pattern, and so will God. Ask for the courage to change it.

The goal isn’t to win an argument. It’s to become a man whose word means what it says, in every room we walk into.
